Canvas

Space is memory.

Every task is a card you place where it belongs. Projects hold their workstreams; related work sits together. You know where things are the way you know where things are in a room, and the colour says what each card needs.

Voice

Push to talk, not terminals.

Hold the space bar and say what you want: a new task, an answer to an agent's question, feedback on a demo. Obeya routes it to the card you are looking at and answers with a short confirmation and an undo. No tabs of terminals to babysit.

Demos

Approve the demo, not the code.

Every change comes back as a narrated screen recording with chapters and a short report. Watch it, approve it or say what to change. Obeya lands the work on main.

What hangs on the wall

Six principles every feature of Obeya answers to.

  • Principle 1

    One surface

    Everything starts on the canvas. Detail unfolds in place and folds back. Nothing opens a new window, and the view never jumps.

  • Principle 2

    Space is memory

    A card stays where you put it, at a size you can read. A small screen shows part of the canvas, never shrunken cards.

  • Principle 3

    Decisions, not supervision

    You are pulled in only for a decision: a demo to approve, a question only you can answer, a proposal. Everything else runs.

  • Principle 4

    Every change gets a demo

    Half a minute for a fix, a few minutes for a feature, never padded. You approve what you saw, not a diff.

  • Principle 5

    Voice first, mouse welcome

    Hold a key and speak, anywhere. A short confirmation answers, written and spoken, with undo. Buttons stay for the obvious.

  • Principle 6

    Your repository, your rules

    What is specific to a project lives in an adapter inside that repository. The core stays general.

The big room

At Toyota, the obeya was the big room where a project lived on the walls. Every plan, every problem, every status hung where anyone could see it, and decisions were made standing in front of the wall. Nobody had to ask how things stood. You looked.

AI agents can now do the work of a team. What they lack is the room. Terminals scroll away, chats forget, and you end up watching agents instead of directing them. Obeya gives the agents a wall to work on and gives you a place to stand in front of it.

Obeya is built with Obeya.

Its own tasks hang on its own wall, and every change to it arrived as a demo first. This page was a card there too.

Get started

Obeya runs locally. The agents use the official Claude Code installation on your machine, with your own subscription.

You need

  • Bun to run Obeya
  • Claude Code, logged in
  • git and gh for clones and pull requests

Then

# get it
git clone https://github.com/sadilek/obeya && cd obeya
# open the wall for one of your repositories
bun install && bun start ~/dev/your-repo

The wall opens on http://127.0.0.1:4417.
