    Canvas

    Space is memory.

    Every task is a card you place where it belongs, and there it stays. Projects hold their workstreams; related work sits together. You know where things are the way you know where things are in a room. The state is a colour, not a column: a card does not move when its agent is done. Each product you build gets a canvas of its own, over one repository or several, and one Obeya runs them all side by side.

    Voice

    Push to talk, not terminals.

    Hold the space bar and say what you want: a new task, an answer to an agent's question, feedback on a demo. Obeya routes it to the card you are looking at. No tabs of terminals to babysit.

    Demos

    Watch the demo instead of testing it yourself.

    Lean back, as in a launch review. Every change comes back with a short report and something to look at: a narrated screen recording with chapters, or a page when the result is a layout or variants side by side. No clicking through the app to see whether it works. Approve it or say what to change, also about one moment of the video: pause it there, and the agent gets that frame with your words.

    Obeya is built with Obeya: see the latest demos from its own development.

    Order

    Parallel where it can, in order where it must.

    Start ten tasks or a whole project at once. Before a card starts, Obeya looks at what it will change and at what is already running. Independent work starts right away; a card that builds on another or would collide with it waits until that one has landed. Point at a card to see what it waits for, or draw an arrow from one card to another to make the second wait for the first.

    Review

    Pull requests from others, reviewed like your own work.

    Say “Review PR #14”, and a pull request someone else opened gets a card. Its agent reads the diff before anything of it runs on your machine, reviews it and records a demo before and after. For each finding you choose: to the contributor, changed by the agent, a follow-up task, or ignore it. You edit the text that goes onto the pull request, then “Push and merge” or “Push and comment only”.

    When the next step is the contributor’s, the card waits until they respond: their comment or push brings it back, reviewed again.

    Phone

    The wall in your pocket.

    Away from the desk, your phone shows what waits for you across all your canvases, grouped by canvas, and what is running. Watch a demo and approve it, answer a question, or hold the microphone and say a new task. A notification tells you when a card needs you.

    Obeya keeps running on your computer and reaches your phone over your own private network, Tailscale or another VPN. Nothing goes through a service of ours. Turn on “Reachable from other devices” in its configuration and scan the code of “Connect a phone”.

    Fits your setup

    • Workers on other machines

      A repository’s agents can also run on other computers, reached over SSH. Obeya clones the repository there itself, and a new card goes there once this machine has no free slot.

    • Folders without git

      A document archive in a cloud drive works too. Cards work in the folder itself, each lists what it changed, and “Discard changes” puts it back.

    • GitHub, Forgejo and Gitea

      Approved work lands on main or goes out as a pull request, which Obeya carries to the merge.

    • English and German

      The interface speaks English and German, and so does the voice input. Agents answer in the language you use.

    The big room

    Obeya is Japanese for the big room where a project lives on the walls. Nobody has to ask how things stand. You look.

    One agent in one chat works well. Scale your work beyond a few, and you lose yourself in parallel chats that know nothing of each other: which one is waiting for you, which one is stuck, which one is touching what another built. Obeya puts every agent's work on one wall instead, and you stand in front of it.

    Get started

    Obeya is an app for macOS, Windows and Linux and runs locally. The agents use the official Claude Code or Codex installation on your machine, with your own subscription.

    Download

    • macOS Apple Silicon Intel
    • Windows Installer (x64)
    • Linux AppImage x64 arm64 .deb x64 arm64

    On its first start Obeya checks for Claude Code or Codex, git and gh, installs what it can and opens your first canvas. The Windows installer is not signed yet (applied for at the SignPath Foundation): when SmartScreen warns, choose “More info”, then “Run anyway”. Code signing policy

    From source

    # needs Bun, Claude Code or Codex (logged in), git and gh
git clone https://github.com/sadilek/obeya && cd obeya
# the same setup, then your first canvas
bun install && bun start

    The wall opens on http://127.0.0.1:4417, through the link Obeya prints, which signs your browser in. The app and the source share their data in ~/.obeya.
